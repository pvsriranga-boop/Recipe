Biryani Recipe

Biryani is a flavorful, aromatic, and richly spiced rice dish that originated in the Indian subcontinent and has since become popular worldwide. It is traditionally made by layering fragrant basmati rice with marinated meat—such as chicken, mutton, beef, fish, or even prawns—or sometimes vegetables, along with a blend of spices like cardamom, cloves, cinnamon, cumin, and saffron. The word “biryani” is believed to come from the Persian words *birian* (fried before cooking) and *birinj* (rice), reflecting its historical Mughal and Persian influences. The cooking method often involves the *dum* technique, where the pot is sealed with dough or a tight lid to trap steam, allowing the rice and meat to cook together slowly so that the flavors fully infuse. Biryani is known for its rich layers of taste and aroma—thanks to ingredients like fried onions, yogurt, fresh herbs, and ghee. There are many regional variations, such as Hyderabadi, Lucknowi (Awadhi), Kolkata, and Malabar biryani, each with its own cooking style and spice profile. It is often served with cooling raita, salad, or boiled eggs, making it a complete, festive meal often enjoyed at weddings, celebrations, and special occasions. How to cook Biryani

To cook biryani, first marinate your meat (chicken, mutton, or fish) with yogurt, spices, and herbs for at least 30 minutes. In a large pot, partially cook basmati rice with whole spices like cardamom, cloves, and bay leaves. Heat oil or ghee in another pan, fry onions until golden, then add the marinated meat and cook until tender. Layer the meat and rice alternately in a heavy-bottomed pot, sprinkle saffron milk or food color, add fried onions and fresh coriander, then seal and cook on low heat (dum) until flavors blend. Serve hot with raita or salad.
